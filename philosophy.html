Core Beliefs

Seven Pillars of My Teaching

01
💡

My Concept of Learning

Learning, to me, is an active personal process. A successful learning situation is one where students feel that it is okay to make mistakes, misunderstand and ask questions. Basically a very safe atmosphere to grow as a learner. I believe learning happens through connecting knowledge or data to the lived experience — not when information is received passively and memorized.

In my classroom, every student is a learner who consistently tries to make meaning and thinks critically. A successful lesson is one where students feel safe to express themselves, having more questions to ask than when they arrived to my class.

02
🧡

My Concept of Teaching

Teaching, for me, is the art of creating conditions in which learning can flourish. My values as a teacher are empathy, honesty, and high expectations for every student. I believe a perfect teaching situation is one where learners feel respected, challenged, and genuinely seen.

The lesson plans I create support students' overall language development in four skills as well as developing their critical thinking and problem solving skills. Course materials are selected to reflect real-world language use. Assignments and assessments are meaningful in a way that they prepare students for real life situations rather than just for a test.

03
🎯

My Goals for Students

My ideal student is a confident, independent communicator who can express ideas clearly in English,both in written and spoken forms, across a range of real-life contexts. Beyond language skills, I want students to develop critical thinking, the ability to collaborate, and a lifelong love of learning. My students use English not just to have casual conversations but also to express their ideas and opinions, to share their experiences and knowledge, and to connect with others in meaningful ways.

The outcomes I care about the most go way beyond just being accurate. I want my students to be able to use English as a tool to open new doors for themselves, to feel confident when they speak their minds, and to connect with people from different cultures in a way that actually changes their lives for the better.

04
🔬

My Teaching Methods

To reach the goals above, I use a blend of communicative language teaching, task-based learning, and collaborative inquiry. Specific strategies I use include: role-plays and simulations, peer feedback in writing workshops, questioning, and multimodal input (videos, podcasts, visual texts) to help different learning styles.

Concrete Example: In an IELTS preparation task, instead of just practicing speaking questions, I set up a "Global Travel Expo" role-play. Students acted as travel consultants and clients, requiring them to use specific academic vocabulary and functional language to negotiate itineraries and solve travel problems in real-time.

For Individualized Education Plan (IEP) students and learners with varied proficiency levels, I scaffold tasks carefully so that access, without lowering expectations, is what I adjust. Grammar is taught in context, which is tied to meaningful communication rather than isolated drills.

05
💬

Interaction with Students

I aim to build real connections with my students by being approachable and genuinely interested in their progress. I see mentoring as a core part of my job, and I want my office hours to be a space for actual conversation, not just a formal requirement. I try to listen more than I talk, knowing that every student has their own unique background.

Classroom management, for me, is built on relationships, not rules. When expectations are clear, when students feel respected, and when the work is meaningful, most behavioral challenges simply do not arise. I model the curiosity, courage, and respect I hope to see in my students.

06
✍️

Assessing Learning

I assess student growth through a combination of formative and summative tools: class participation, drafts and revisions, oral presentations, quizzes, tests and performance tasks. Feedback is given as dialogue — specific, actionable, and encouraging.

Concrete Example: When a student struggled with academic writing, specifically using complex sentences, instead of just marking "error," I provided a "sentence-building bridge" feedback. I showed them their original simple sentence and a possible complex version, asking them to try another one in their next draft. This turned assessment into a coaching moment.

I care more about how much a student grows than how perfect their work is. I don't just give a grade; I look at the effort and progress behind it. I want my students to see mistakes as a way to learn, not as a reason to be afraid. This helps them feel more confident and less anxious whenever they are being assessed.

07
🚀

Professional Growth

I believe the best teachers never stop being students. I’m always looking for ways to improve, whether that’s by reading up on the latest research, attending teaching workshops, or exploring how new tools like AI are changing the way we learn.

I know I’ve done my job when students start taking charge of their own learning. One of my favorite moments is when a student feels confident enough to disagree with me and can explain why—that’s when I know they’re really thinking for themselves.

In the Classroom

How I Actually Teach

I start every class by checking in. I want to know how my students are doing before we dive into the lesson. Once we're ready, I give them a real challenge—something like a problem to solve or a story to talk about—to get them thinking.

I keep things interesting by using a mix of group projects, speaking activities, and writing tasks. I also bring in videos and podcasts because I know everyone learns differently, and I want to make sure everyone can follow along.

I try to show my students how much I love learning. I'm honest about my own journey with language, and if I don't have an answer to a question, I'm happy to say, "I'm not sure—let's look it up together."
